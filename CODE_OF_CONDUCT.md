Code of conduct
The short version

Be decent. Assume the person you're replying to is trying.

What's expected
Criticise the work, not the person. "This calculation is wrong because…" is welcome and useful. "You clearly don't know what you're doing" is neither.
Assume competence and good faith. People arrive with different backgrounds. Someone asking a basic question is not wasting your time.
Accept that people communicate differently. Some contributors are direct to the point of bluntness; some need extra time or written detail rather than a call; some won't pick up on hints. None of that is rudeness. Read for content, not tone.
Take the correction. Being wrong in public is normal here — half the material on these projects is published specifically so it can be corrected.
What isn't acceptable
Harassment, personal attacks, or sustained hostility.
Discrimination or demeaning comments relating to disability, neurotype, race, gender, sexuality, religion, nationality, or age. This applies with particular force to anything touching autism or ADHD, given what some of these projects are about.
Publishing someone's private information.
Deliberately derailing threads, or persistently reopening a settled decision.
Scope

Applies to every repository in this organisation — issues, pull requests, discussions and commit messages — and to anywhere someone is representing the project.

Enforcement

Report to tj@divergentfutures.co. Reports are handled privately.

Responses escalate as needed: a note, a warning, removing a comment, or blocking someone from the organisation. There's no committee — one person reads it and decides. That's a limitation, and it's stated plainly rather than dressed up as a process.
